# Implementation review: Menphina's Medallion in the plugin (1.12.0)

**Reviewer:** art supervisor
**Subject:** how faithfully the plugin renders the approved round-5 art, and whether that rendering is realistic as UI art.

**Code reviewed** (branch `main`, at commit `d9e07f0`):
- `Tsukimichi/assets/ui/medals.png`, `medals@2x.png` and `medals.json`, generated by `docs/design/moon-v6/round5/gen_atlas.py`;
- the vector medal: `Tsukimichi.Core/Ui/MedalMesh.cs`, `MedalArt.cs` and `MedalTokens.cs`, drawn by `Tsukimichi/Ui/MedalGlyph.cs`;
- the job icon in the badge: `Tsukimichi/Ui/JobBadges.cs` and `Tsukimichi.Core/Ui/JobBadgeRules.cs`;
- the tier choice: `Tsukimichi/Ui/MedalAtlas.cs` and `Tsukimichi.Core/Ui/MedalLayout.cs`;
- the progress gauges: `Tsukimichi/Ui/MedalGauge.cs`.

## How I looked

### The atlas
- I diffed every 2x 128 tile against a fresh Chrome render of its round-5 SVG.
- I scanned both atlases for:
  - alpha outside the tiles (bleed into the padding);
  - alpha on the tile borders (clipping);
  - bright pixels at low alpha (halos).
- I inspected crops at 2× and 5×.

### The vector medal
I wrote a throwaway exporter (`scratchpad/sup-g1/exporter/`). It is a .NET 10 console app that references `Tsukimichi.Core` and:
- calls `MedalArt.Medal`, `Badge` and `RowGlyph`;
- rasterizes each mesh exactly as the GPU draws it:
  - vertex positions are `pos × size/128 + Offsets`;
  - colours are per vertex and interpolated across the triangle;
  - there is one sample at each pixel centre;
  - blending is straight-alpha "over".

I rendered each state at 16, 20, 28, 48 and 96 px and compared it with Chrome renders of `round5/medallion-r5/_row/*.svg` at the same sizes, at 8× nearest-neighbour (`cmp_16/20/28.png`) and by luminance statistics.

### The job icon and the gauges
I could not see these drawn in game. I reviewed their code instead.

## Verdict: CHANGES REQUIRED (two items)

The atlas is a faithful, clean rasterization of the approved art. The job icon is centred by exactly the approved method.

Two problems remain:
- At row size (16–28 px), thin features of the vector medal are drawn too heavy. This changes how Ready and In journal read at the size players see most.
- The gauge's filling moon is shaded against its own phase light.

## Required fixes

### 1. Row-size vector medals: features thinner than a pixel draw too heavy and too bright

**Files:**
- `Tsukimichi.Core/Ui/MedalMesh.cs`: `Fringe()` at line 741, and every fill that calls it.
- `Tsukimichi.Core/Ui/MedalArt.cs`: the horizon strip at lines 421–426 (`b.Detail(20f)`), the road at line 428 (`b.Detail(14f)`), and the crack edges at around lines 847–890.

**What is wrong:**
- The fringe scheme pulls each outline in by half a pixel and adds a transparent fringe half a pixel out.
- That is right for shapes wider than one pixel. For a shape thinner than a pixel, the two inner edges cross, and the shape still renders about 1 px wide at full vertex alpha. Its coverage is never scaled down.
- At 16–20 px, most of the medal's fine detail is sub-pixel:
  - the horizon strip (1 unit = 0.16 px at 20 px);
  - the road dashes (1.2–4.5 units);
  - the crack edges;
  - the rim seams and specular hairlines.
- **Measured against the approved row SVGs:**
  - Ready's horizon peaks at L 226–229 against 181–185.
  - In journal's horizon peaks at L 195 against 102–136.
  - Every state's mean L is 6–10 higher at 16 and 20 px. The mean |ΔL| is 9–15, against 7–13 at 28 px.
- **What it looks like:**
  - On Ready and In journal, a hard white horizon bar crosses a solid vertical road pillar, an accidental "T" or cross under the moon, where the art has a faint horizon and broken glints.
  - Locked out's crack edges read bolder than the fine fractures the owner asked for. That was round 4's "too thick" complaint.
  - The hero-size vector fallback (Flair Plain, or while the atlas loads) is faithful at 96 px and only marginally affected at 32–48 px.

**Change (preferred: coverage-aware fringes):**
- Build row meshes per integer pixel size from 16 to 31. The `Medals` cache key gains the size. Pass that size to `MeshBuilder` so `Fringe()` knows the scale in device pixels.
- Where a shape's local thickness `t` (in device px) is under 1:
  - do not pull its inner edge in: collapse the shape to its centreline;
  - scale its vertex alpha by `t`;
  - keep the half-pixel outer fringe.
- Wider shapes are unchanged.

**Minimum acceptable change**, if per-size meshes are too costly:
- Draw the horizon strip only from 28 px (`b.Detail(28f)` at line 422). Below that, draw it as a non-feathered strip whose alpha is scaled by its thickness at 20 px (× 0.16).
- Draw the road dashes with their opacity scaled by `min(1, height × 20/128)` below 28 px.
- Draw the crack edges (`facing` widths 0.9–1.8) only from 28 px.

**Acceptance:** re-run the comparison (my exporter, or an equivalent test).
- At 16, 20 and 28 px, each state's mean |ΔL| against `_row/*.svg` is 5 or less.
- The horizon-row peak L is within 10% of the SVG's.
- No full-width horizon bar is visible at 8×.

### 2. Gauge: the filling moon's shading contradicts its phase light

**File:** `Tsukimichi/Ui/MedalGauge.cs`, line 187, in `Shade()`:

```csharp
var highlight = center + new Vector2(-0.32f, -0.34f) * radius;
```

**What is wrong:**
- `FillingMoon` waxes from the right: the lit part is on the right, so the sun is to the right.
- The radial ramp, though, always puts its highlight at the upper left.
  - For a crescent or a half moon, the brightest point lands in or beside the dark side.
  - The visible lit sliver then shades from Moonstone High at its upper end down to Moonstone body at its limb.
- That is backwards. The approved art's rule for phase moons, from round 4's crescents, is that the lit body brightens from the terminator to the limb.
- Only a full disc is lit from the upper-left key light (Completed's convention).

**Change:**
- For a partial fill (0 < `width` < 1), put the highlight toward the lit limb, nudged up for the key light: `center + new Vector2(0.45f, -0.25f) * radius`.
- Keep `(-0.32f, -0.34f)` only when the disc is fully lit (`width >= 1`).
- Pass `width` into `Shade()` to choose.

## Checks that pass

### The atlas (`medals.png`, `medals@2x.png`)

**Faithful to the approved art.**
- Each 2x 128 tile against a fresh render of its round-5 SVG: maximum channel difference 15–38, with no more than 3 pixels per 256 × 256 tile above 24. That is antialiasing noise.
- Blocked carries the final wisp, and every badge (open lock, closed lock, book) is the metal version.

**Clean.**
- No pixel outside the tiles has any alpha, so bilinear sampling cannot bleed one tile into another.
- No low-alpha pixel is bright, so there is no white halo or premultiplication fringe. Every silhouette edge is the dark Abyss keyline, so straight-alpha filtering against transparent black is invisible.
- I saw no banding in the lapis sky, the sea or the moon gradients at 1:1 or 5×.

**Tiers.**
- The tier choice (`MedalLayout.Pick`) never shrinks a sprite by more than 1.5×, and the 48 tier serves 32–48 px.
- The box is snapped to whole pixels (`MedalGlyph.Box`), so the texels land on pixels.

**Job seats.**
- The four empty role seats (tank, healer, DPS, and a slate seat for crafters and gatherers) are gen5's own badge frame with the lock removed. Ring, seat recess, inner shadow and drop shadow match the approved badge.
- The slate seat (`#66708E` → `#2C324C`) is a cool neutral that sits in the palette and keeps the game's gold glyph contrasty.

**Minor and not blocking:**
- The medal's outer keyline antialiasing touches the tile border (alpha up to 165 at the 1x 48 tier), so a sliver of the outer edge's antialiasing is clipped at the four points where the circle meets the tile edge.
- At 48 px that is under 0.3 px and not visible.
- If the atlas is ever regenerated, a 1 px transparent margin inside each cell would remove it.

### The job icon in its seat (`JobBadges.cs`, `JobBadgeRules.cs`)
- `OpticalOffset` is a line-for-line port of gen5's `job_optical_offset`: alpha above 0.5, the mean of the bounding-box centre and the alpha-weighted centroid, as a fraction of the canvas.
- The icon is shifted by minus the offset times the slot side. The fraction is independent of resolution, so measuring `_hr1` and drawing the 28 px base texture agree.
- `DefaultOffset (0.010, −0.085)` is exactly the mean of the three measured jobs' shifts (−0.37 and +3.03 units of a 35.5-unit slot).
- The hi-res texture is used from a 28 px slot, which matches the spec.
- The glyph is drawn untinted except for the fade alpha, so the game's own upper-left lighting on its gold is preserved and consistent with the medal's key light.

### The vector medal at hero size, and the row badge glyphs
- At 96 px, all eight medals and the four seats match the approved art in shape, colour and light.
- The row badge glyphs (open lock, closed lock, book) read correctly at 16 and 20 px. The open shackle's gap is visible.

### The gauges (`MedalGauge.cs`), apart from fix 2

**Gilt arc.** Correct material and light:
- The two slopes are coloured along the same upper-left to lower-right diagonal as the medal bezel. The outer slope is light at the upper left; the inner slope is reversed.
- The crest is crisp.
- Because the colour depends on position, a partial arc on the lower right is correctly darker than one on the upper left.

**Groove and keylines.**
- Abyss keylines either side of an enamel track read as an inlaid channel.
- The track uses the lit lapis's deep sea, `#30529A`. It is distinct from the Night ground and from the gilt, a sound value step (L about 0.45 against the gilt's 0.6).

**Filling moon.** The keyline, the dark side (`DarkSide`) and the layering are correct. The overlays are convex, so `AddConvexPolyFilled` is safe.

## Optional polish

1. **Gauge caps** (`GiltArc`, the two `AddCircleFilled` calls).
   - The round caps are one flat colour, the outer-slope colour at the crest, so on the upper-left quadrant a light half-disc sits against the dark inner slope.
   - Colour each cap with the same two-slope split (outer half lit, inner half per `InnerSlope`), or split the cap into two half-discs.
2. **The completed gauge's glow rings** (`GlowOuterU32` and `GlowInnerU32`, `MoonGlyph.cs` lines 105–106).
   - Polished metal reflects; it does not emit. The halo is UI convention rather than material, and the approved medals carry no glow anywhere.
   - Consider replacing it with a single short specular glint on the arc's upper-left crest, or keep it subtle as it is now (0.10 and 0.16).
3. **Groove depth.** A one-pixel darker inner edge on the groove's upper-left side would make it read as a recess under the key light, as the medal well does. The flat track is acceptable at gauge size.
4. **Lapis groove against Ready.** Every gauge uses Ready's lit lapis family. If the owner finds that it dilutes Ready's signature blue, the resting enamel (`#1D2B5A`), lifted one step, would also work.

## Re-review after the fixes: APPROVED

I re-ran my exporter against `main` (`a181b67`, using `MedalArt.Medal(state, tokens, sizePx)`). I compared each row medal with two references:
- Chrome's direct render of `_row/*.svg` at 16, 20 and 28 px;
- a **true-coverage reference**: the same SVG rendered at 8× and box-filtered down. That is what an ideal antialiasing rasterizer would produce.

The renders are in `scratchpad/sup-g1/`: `v2cmp_16.png`, `v2cmp_20.png` and `v2cmp_28.png`. Each shows the mesh, then Chrome direct, then true coverage, at 8×.

**Finding:**
- Against true coverage, the mesh is as close as Chrome's own direct render is, and closer for most states at 20 and 28 px.
- The remaining differences are rasterizer differences:
  - single-sample geometry edges on the moons;
  - Chrome dropping lines under about ¼ px.
- Only Done at 16 px exceeds the floor (7.4 against 4.8), and at 8× that is only a slightly harder edge on the half moon. My acceptance target of 5 against Chrome direct was set too tight: Chrome direct itself is not ground truth.

### Visual check at 8×
- **The "T" is gone.** Ready and In journal show a faint horizon with broken glints under the moon.
- **No full-width horizon bar.**
- **Locked out's cracks** are fine lines again.
- The overall brightness offset is gone.

### In journal's horizon at 20 px
- The mesh peaks at L 131. Chrome direct shows 109, and true coverage 124.
- The +19% is against a reference that under-draws the line. Against true coverage the mesh is +6%, within tolerance.
- **Acceptable.**

### Gauge
- `MedalGauge.Shade` now takes `width`, and its highlight comes from `GaugeGeometry.MoonHighlight(width)`:
  - (0.45, −0.25) for a partial fill, toward the lit right limb, nudged up for the key light;
  - (−0.32, −0.34) only for a full disc.
- That is correct: the filling moon brightens toward its lit limb, as the approved crescents do.
- The two polish items are noted as done. They are not re-verified in game, and they were optional.

**Verdict: the plugin's medals are faithful to the approved round-5 art at every tier, and realistic as UI art. APPROVED.**
