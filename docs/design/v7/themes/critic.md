# v7 themes: design critic review

Reviewer: the design critic (meaning, beauty, FFXIV feel, consistency, mixability, taste). Light and physics belong to the realism supervisor and are not judged here.

Inputs: `docs/research/plan-v7/theme-system.md`, the three `concept.md` files and sheets, the shipped `moon-v6/round5/medallion-r5/` sheets, and round 2's `FINDINGS.md` and sheets. I also rendered all four sets side by side at 150 px and 300 px, and every row tier at 16, 20 and 32 px on Night (#0F1424) and Ishgard Snow (#EEF1F6). I upscaled the row renders 3x with pixelated scaling and built three mixed eight-state columns plus a "risk" row. The renders are in the session scratchpad (`critic-v7/`), not committed.

## Verdict in one paragraph

All three are clearly better than their round 2 selves. Every round 2 stock-icon failure is gone: no no-entry sign, no hamburger, no Saturn, no coin, no lollipop and no AI-sparkle crystal. All three now speak the round 5 grammar.

- **Ishgard Glass** is the one that earns a separate theme. It is the most distinct from Medallion, the most FFXIV, and it holds up at row size.
- **Aether Crystal** is calm, cool and distinct, but its Blocked breaks the grammar and creates the worst mixing hazard in the set.
- **Astrologian's Orrery** has the two most beautiful single glyphs of the round (the constellation "?" and the scroll-cloud). As a whole theme, though, it is Medallion with stars. In the side-by-side render, its Ready, Done, Completed and Locked out are hard to tell from Medallion's.

There is also one cross-cutting defect: **all three bake their own frame into every SVG.** The theme system (§3.2, §3.3) requires unframed faces and one frame kit per mix. Until that is split, every mix shows two or three rim materials in one column, which is exactly the owner's round 2 complaint.

## Ishgard Glass: "the Holy See's rose window"

**Better than round 2?** Yes, by the widest margin of the three.
- The came now survive at 16 px: the 3.4-unit row lead and the pale stone ring make every state read as leaded glass, not as a plain disc.
- The no-entry Locked out is now a shattered ruby with the lead still holding. That is the best idea in any v7 set.
- The abraded flashed-glass road is a genuinely clever material answer, and it cannot become a fir tree or paving stones.

**Reads without a legend?** Mostly.
- Blocked (two leaded clouds, with the front bank low and wide) reads at every size.
- Not checked holds on daylight now.

Stock-read risks:
- **Done this cycle at hero:** the half moon sits on a field of radial spokes inside a circular arrow. That is the Material "history" icon (a clock with a wrap-around arrow), and the spokes also make the half moon read as a pie chart.
- **Locked out at 16–20 px:** the fallen-out pane is a black wedge that reaches the rim on the right, so the glyph reads as a pie with a slice missing (Pac-Man). At 32 px and up it reads correctly as a void.
- **Ready at 48 px:** nine spokes, the halo ring and a three-light crescent make it busy, as the designer's own doubts say. The leaded full disc also echoes round 2's "lemon slice".

**Distinct from Medallion?** Yes, the most distinct of the three. A cool stone ring, visible lead, and glass that glows from behind instead of being lit from the front. It is worth its own theme, and it is the natural partner for the first light palette (Ishgard Snow).

**FFXIV?** Strong in concept (the Holy See, Coerthas, the Dragonsong era), but under-delivered.
- The plan (§4.2) asks for the **Ishgard skyline on Ready's far shore at hero tier**. Round 2's jurors called that skyline the best FFXIV detail of the round, and it was dropped.
- Without it, the set is "a cathedral window", not "Ishgard's window".

**Best glyph:** Blocked. It is material-true, readable at 16 px, and the low front bank owns a region no other state lights.

**Worst glyph:** Done this cycle (the clock and pie-chart read above).

## Aether Crystal: "moonstone in a silver bezel"

**Better than round 2?** Yes.
- The no-entry Locked out is now a cleaved red crystal with a shard falling away, and the strongest Locked out of the three.
- The hamburger is now a "?" whose bowl is a moon's limb, with a tiny full moon as its dot.
- The coin is now a faceted full moon with a gilt check through the rim.
- The lollipop is gone; the road now sits inside the well.
- The teal mobile-game glow is gone.

The set is calm and cool, with no cheese.

**Reads without a legend?** No, because of one glyph.
- **Blocked is a bare crescent:** a thin, bright C on a dark, faceted disc, with no cloud and no obscuring mass. Three problems follow:
  1. **It breaks the grammar.** §2 requires the new moon to be obscured, and §4.1 specified a "faceted frost bank".
  2. **It is a stock read.** It is the iOS and Android "Do Not Disturb / night mode" moon.
  3. **It collides with every crescent "Ready on another job":** its own, Medallion's, Orrery's and Ishgard's (see Mixing). The metrics confirm it: RoJ-Blk is the set's weakest pair, at 12.1 on the row tier.
- **Done this cycle:** the arrow is the brightest, most saturated blue in the set, with the largest arrowhead. Of all four sets' Done glyphs, it reads most like a refresh icon at 16–20 px. The designer flags this too.
- **Completed at 16–20 px:** the 33-facet cut becomes speckle, which the owner would call noise.
- **Dark side of Ready on another job and Blocked:** the brilliant cut's 8-point star of table facets shows clearly at hero. It is not the banned 4-point sparkle, but it is a star motif in a moon's shadow, and it competes with the earthshine.

**Distinct from Medallion?** Yes, by material and silhouette: the 16-gon cut bezel, cool moonstone and no warm metal. It is worth offering, because it is the "calm, cool" theme Medallion can't be.
- The bezel colour is periwinkle-lavender (#5868A8 to #A2B2DC). Next to brass it looks like plastic. The plan's Silver kit is a moonstone silver (#E2E8F4 to #5E6E97), which would read as metal.

**FFXIV?** Moderate.
- Crystal is the game's central motif, but a Western brilliant cut seen from above is jewellery, not FFXIV crystal. The game's crystals are elongated, hexagonal prisms with pointed ends, and they carry aether.
- The aether current in Done's arrow is the set's one real FFXIV note.

**Best glyph:** Locked out. The material and the meaning coincide: a crystal cleaves, and Dalamud is broken.

**Worst glyph:** Blocked.

## Astrologian's Orrery: "a Sharlayan instrument"

**Better than round 2?** Yes. The sun-ray ticks, the lightbulb Ready, Saturn, the coin and the stray slash are all gone.

**Reads without a legend?** Yes. It has the best metrics of the three (weakest pair 13.6), and every state reads.
- **Blocked's brass kumo** reads as a cloud, but because it is bright gilt it reads as a "partly sunny" weather icon. More importantly, it puts a large warm gold mass on a resting state.
  - In the grammar, gilt means "act now".
  - At 16 px, Blocked is the second warmest and second loudest glyph in the row (salience 45, against 42 for Medallion's grey cloud).
  - With the ember ribbon on In journal, the row has three warm marks besides Ready.
- **Not checked at hero:** the constellation "?" is lovely. At a stretch it could read as a molecule or network diagram, but the "?" shape is strong enough.

**Distinct from Medallion?** No, and that is its problem.
- At 150 px, Ready, Done, Completed and Locked out are Medallion's glyphs on a slightly greener blue, in a slightly darker brass rim. The designer says so: "Ready is the state where the two themes differ least: palette and rim only".
- At 16–20 px the two rows are almost interchangeable, except for the gold cloud and the orange ribbon.
- A theme card that looks like the default with stars is a weak option in a picker.

**FFXIV?** Good where it is distinct.
- The constellation "?" is the Astrologian's own visual language.
- The star-atlas book badge is charming.
- The kumo is Hingashi (Kugane), not Sharlayan, so the idioms are mixed.

**Best glyph:** Not checked, the constellation "?". It is the best Not checked of all four sets at hero, and the only one with real character.

**Worst glyph:** Ready. It is identical to Medallion's in all but palette, and so gives no reason to choose the theme.

## Ranking

1. **Ishgard Glass.** The most distinct, the most FFXIV, and survives at row size. Its fixes are refinements.
2. **Aether Crystal.** Distinct and calm. One structural fix (Blocked), then polish.
3. **Astrologian's Orrery.** The best individual glyphs, the weakest case for a whole theme.

## Which two ship first

**Ishgard Glass and Aether Crystal**, as the plan proposes (1.14), on two conditions:
- **Aether Crystal's Blocked is redrawn** (change 5) before it goes to the owner.
- **Both are split into face and frame layers** (change 1) before any mixing ships.

**The Orrery should not ship as a separate theme in its current form.** Either:
- **(a)** give it a real instrument identity across all eight states (change 13), or
- **(b)** drop the whole theme and offer its Not checked (and a recoloured Blocked) as mix faces from 1.15.

I recommend (b) unless the owner especially wants an Astrologian theme.

## Concrete changes

**All three**

1. **Split faces from frames.** Each `_src` generator (`aether-crystal/_src/gen_ac.py` `bezel()`, `ishgard-glass/_src/gen.py` `frame()`, `astrologian-orrery/_src/gen.py` `limb()`) must emit the face (well plus emblem, round well, the shared r 63.2 silhouette) separately. The bezel goes to `v7/themes/kits/<kit>/`, at four urgency tiers with a gilt act-now tier.
   - Today Ready's frame in Aether and Ishgard is the same moon-quartz or stone as every other state. Ready's lead comes from its bright scene alone, against the §3.3 "act now is gilt in every kit" rule.
   - The Aether 16-gon becomes the Silver kit's frame shape. The face underneath stays round, so it sits in any kit.
2. **Badges move to the kit layer as well.** Each generator's `badge()` call currently renders the ring in the theme's material. Keep the content glyphs per kit, as §3.3 says, and keep the badge-ring geometry identical (it already is).

**Ishgard Glass**

3. **`ishgard-glass/_src/gen.py` `done()`: drop the `night_field(... spokes=...)` spokes** behind Done (pass `spokes=()`), or keep at most two faint ones that don't radiate from the moon's centre. This removes the clock and pie-chart ("history" icon) read.
4. **`ishgard-glass/_src/gen.py` `locked_out()`, the `gone = 2` light:**
   - Choose an inner shard that does not reach the limb, so the void sits inside the ring of shards rather than as a rim wedge. Or shrink the void to roughly half its area.
   - At 16 px it must read as a hole, not as a missing pie slice.
5. **`ishgard-glass/_src/gen.py` `moon_window()` (Ready, hero tier only): add the Ishgard skyline** on the far shore, as §4.2 asks:
   - a single dark-glass silhouette of the Vault's spires, leaded on its outline, about 6 units tall;
   - placed left of the road, clear of the badge disc;
   - with the halo rose cut to seven spokes, as the designer suggested, to pay for the detail.

   This is the set's FFXIV signature, and its absence is the biggest loss from round 2.
6. **`ishgard-glass/_src/gen.py` `in_journal()`:** add the same skyline at night, at low contrast (hero only), so the two scene states share it.

**Aether Crystal**

7. **`aether-crystal/_src/gen_ac.py` `blocked()`: add the faceted frost bank** the plan specified.
   - It is low-poly cloud in the set's own moonstone, three or four flat facets.
   - It covers the lower half of the disc and is wider than it, in the lower well.
   - It sits at a value between the well and the limb, so the limb stays the only light.
   - This restores the grammar, kills the Do Not Disturb read, and separates Blocked from every crescent "Ready on another job" (RoJ) by a mass in a region no RoJ lights.
8. **`gen_ac.py` `done()`, the arrow:**
   - drop its value about 15% and its chroma toward the moonstone (keep the aether hue only on the inner edge);
   - shorten the arrowhead's span to about 13 units (from the round of 17 that most sets use).

   It should read as a current, not a button.
9. **`gen_ac.py` `facet_moon()` on the dark side** (the `earth=` ramp used by `ready_other_job()` and `blocked()`):
   - halve the contrast between table, star and kite facets in earthshine;
   - let only the girdle catch the gloss.

   The 8-point star should disappear below 64 px.
10. **`gen_ac.py` `completed()` row tier:** render the row face (`_row/completed.svg`) with the table and the kites only (about 9 facets), not 33, so 16–20 px reads as a stone, not as speckle.
11. **Silver kit colour:** move the bezel ramp from the periwinkle `#5868A8`/`#A2B2DC` toward the plan's moonstone silver `#5E6E97` → `#E2E8F4`. Lower the chroma so it reads as metal beside brass and lead.

**Astrologian's Orrery**

12. **`astrologian-orrery/_src/gen.py` `kumo()` (Blocked): recast the cloud** in blued steel (the closed lock's `#C2CBE0`/`#8C99BA`/`#4E5A80`), or in a patinated dark bronze at most 0.55 of the brass value. Keep the scroll spirals, which are good.
    - Gilt on a resting state contradicts "gilt means act now".
    - It also makes Blocked the loudest glyph after Ready.
13. **If the Orrery stays a whole theme, give every state the instrument:**
    - a faint engraved rete (an ecliptic ring and two star pointers) behind every scene at mid and hero tier, in `moon_scene()` and `plate()`;
    - the cardinal notches promoted to a visible two-tone scale band in `limb()`;
    - a Prussian plate held a step greener than Medallion's lapis.

    The test: at 28 px, a stranger must be able to point to the Orrery row without the label.
14. **`gen.py` `in_journal()`:** the ember ribbon is the set's only orange. Keep it, but take its value down a step (`#E3A060` → about `#C98A52`), so it stays below Ready's warmth.

## Mixing

### Pairings that look bad (seen in the mixed renders)

1. **Aether Blocked with any set's "Ready on another job".** Two dark discs with a thin bright crescent in the same column. With Medallion's or Orrery's RoJ, at 16 px, the only difference is the rim colour. This is the worst pairing in the matrix.
2. **Orrery Blocked with Aether or Ishgard Ready** (on the Silver or Came kits). The gold cloud out-warms a Ready whose frame isn't gilt, so Ready stops being the loudest. Adding Orrery's In journal makes it worse.
3. **Any mix under today's baked frames.** For example, Medallion Ready, Ishgard RoJ, Aether In journal and Orrery Blocked show gilt, stone, periwinkle and brass rims in one column, and a round silhouette beside a 16-gon. It looks like parts from different apps.
4. **Aether Completed among smooth-faced sets.** It is the only textured face, so it sparkles as noise at row size and draws more attention than a finished state should.
5. **Ready from one set and "Ready on another job" from another.** For example, Aether's faceted Ready with Medallion's smooth RoJ. The meaning "the same moon, just not on this job" depends on the two being the same moon. Split across sets, the RoJ reads as a different state, not as a lesser Ready.
6. **Ishgard Locked out next to Ishgard Done** (until changes 3 and 4 land). Both read as pie charts at 16–20 px.

### Rules that prevent them

- **R1, one frame layer.** Faces never carry a frame or a badge ring. Frames come from the one kit chosen for the whole mix, and act-now is gilt in every kit. This is a build gate: a face SVG with pixels outside the well radius fails. It fixes pairings 2 (partly) and 3.
- **R2, Ready and RoJ move together.** The mix table offers one row, "Ready (and Ready on another job)", not two. It fixes pairing 5 and removes the 7 pairs most likely to cross-collide.
- **R3, the gilt budget.** On every face except Ready, saturated gold may appear only as the check (Completed) or the "comes back" arc (Done), and at most ⅙ of the face. This makes G5 a mix-time rule, not only a per-set one. It fixes pairing 2 and change 12 enforces it.
- **R4, Blocked shows its obscurer.** Every set's Blocked must cover at least 30% of the well's lower half with an obscuring mass (cloud, bank or leaded panes). This makes "new moon with a limb" never the whole sign. It is a grammar line in §2 and a build gate, and it fixes pairing 1.
- **R5, row texture ceiling.** Row-tier faces may have no more than about 12 internal edges (facets, came or engraving) inside the moon. A build count on the `_row` masters fixes pairing 4.
- The matrix warnings (§5.2) stay as the safety net for anything these rules miss. With R1 to R5 in place, though, the pure presets and the obvious mixes should never warn.
